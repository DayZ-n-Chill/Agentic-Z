# Winding diagnostics

How each winding state reads with Check A, why offline checks never clear a spawn, how to turn a Check B result into a safe fix, and where offline checks stop deciding the render side. Read `winding-methodology.md` first for Checks B and C, the misleading heuristics and the import checklist; how to read py3d's own winding findings is in `winding-absolute-check.md`.

The cases here were measured on mod models (a wooden crate, a wall lamp, a test car's co-driver door, three gun rack models, a helicopter, the giant infected of `_shared/wearables/references/body-rigging.md`), in game and offline with py3d. Vanilla models on `P:\` are binarized, so none of this can be read from them, and it is not checked on `P:\`.

The Blender to DayZ map and the per component winding check are the sections of those names in `_shared/models/generated-model-rules.md`. After the map, both the cross product `cross(v1-v0, v2-v0)` and the stored normals point away from the side meant to be seen; the per component check requires every face of a closed, convex collision component to read inward.

## Check A: winding against the stored normals

For each face, take `n_winding = normalize(cross(v1-v0, v2-v0))` and compare it with the normalized average of `face.vertices[i].normal` over its corners. On a quad that is not planar and convex, take `n_winding` from the face's vector area instead, the sum of `cross(v[j] − v[0], v[j+1] − v[0])` over its fan: its first three corners can point the other way. The share of faces at `dot < -0.5` counts the faces that strongly disagree with their normals; it does not say which way either points. Leave `proxy:*` triangles out of this count and out of every fix below: their vertex order encodes the proxy frame.

- **About 0 %, UNIFORM_NON_FLIPPED.** No face strongly disagrees. Count agreement too (`dot > 0`): only about 100 % agreement means winding and normals agree, because normals turned 90 to 120 degrees from their faces also read 0 % here. py3d's absolute check counts agreement on the first corner's normal only, so on smoothed normals the two counts can differ, and a face whose corner normals point opposite ways is a defect to inspect, not a sign to flip. A correct export through the Blender to DayZ map reads this with about 100 % agreement, like shipped models (`winding-absolute-check.md`, "What 0 % agreement means"). So do a mirror (determinant +1) with reversed faces and negated normals, and an inside-out model with both outward. Severity NOTE: consistent with the map, not proof of it.
- **About 100 %, UNIFORM_FLIPPED.** Winding and normals disagree. The older outward-normal convention reads this way: cross product inward, normals outward, as in the recipe the giant infected shipped with (`_shared/wearables/references/body-export.md`, section "Winding and normals"). It renders solid but is not what the map writes; a wooden crate mod in this state rendered, took bullets and the action cursor, and collided in game. A correct export whose faces were all reversed afterwards also reads this way, and is inside out. Severity WARNING on a new export.
- **5 to 95 %, MIXED.** Parts in different states, not a verdict by itself. The test car's door, right in game, reads MIXED: 72.67 % of 11,921 faces at `dot < -0.5`, 25.9 % agreeing. Check B finds 1 of its 17,542 two-face edges traversed the same way by both faces (a paint triangle against a black trim one); with that edge cut, each of its 23 welded components orients as one group, so no face group is wound against its neighbours, and that seam is left to inspect. The parts vote apart: exterior paint, black trim and mirror read 94 to 100 % of their faces flipped (the older convention, which renders solid), while cabin plastic, leather, metal and glass read 97.9 to 100 % agreeing. Coincident twins are 144 faces and do not change the count. Never reverse the LOD on this reading: find any group wound against its component with Check B ("From Check B to fix", steps 1 and 2), split the count by part (welded component, then material), and take each part's repair from the table below, each face read as step 3 reads it. Severity WARNING; a visible part the table reads inside out is the defect, its urgency set by visibility.

Neither uniform label is a fix instruction. First decide, part by part, whether the cross product points away from the side meant to be seen (into the material, which on a solid seen from outside is inward) or toward it:

- Visual LOD: the signed volume by winding of each closed shell. Weld points by position, then link two faces only through an edge exactly those two faces share; parts that only touch (a common vertex, or an edge used by more than two faces) stay separate shells, and a shell with an open edge has no meaningful sign. Negative means away from a viewer outside, the sign of a shipped solid (`winding-absolute-check.md`, "Reading rules", rule 4); positive means away from a viewer inside, as a room meant to be seen from inside reads. Never decide on the sum over the LOD, or on a union you cannot split into closed shells: it can hide an inverted part, and reversing it can move the inversion onto a healthy one.
- Collision LODs: the per component winding check. Their whole LOD sign is mixed even on shipped models (rule 7 of the same file).
- Open sheets and double-sided parts: no sign decides; use the visibility check of "From Check B to fix", step 2.

Then fix the side that is wrong, part by part. A normal shared with a face you leave alone gets a new pool entry (step 3 below).

| Winding against stored normals | Cross product | State | To reach the map's convention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agree: UNIFORM_NON_FLIPPED and about 100 % agreement | away from the visible side | correct | nothing |
| Agree | toward the visible side | inside out, normals toward the viewer too | `face.vertices.reverse()` on every face of the part and negate its normals |
| Disagree: UNIFORM_FLIPPED, or about 0 % agreement | away from the visible side | normals toward the viewer: the older outward-normal convention | keep the winding, negate the normals (`winding-absolute-check.md`, rule 5) |
| Disagree | toward the visible side | a correct export with every face reversed | `face.vertices.reverse()` on every face of the part, keep the normals |

A mirror lands in the same row as its unmirrored twin, so whatever the row, check chirality on an asymmetric feature.

Measured offline on the three MLODs of the in-game test of the Blender to DayZ map, rebuilt by the py3d test `test_s7_blender_to_dayz.py` under `scripts/tools/py3d/tests/`: Check A reads UNIFORM_NON_FLIPPED (48 of 48 faces agree) on the correct export, the mirrored one and the inside-out one, with signed volumes −0.1086, −0.1086 and +0.1086. Built with the giant infected's recipe, and as the correct export with every face reversed, the model reads UNIFORM_FLIPPED (0 of 48 agree) at −0.1086 and +0.1086. Two variants show what a label or a whole LOD sum misses: with one of the four boxes reversed and its normals negated, the export still reads UNIFORM_NON_FLIPPED with a negative sum (−0.0822), while that box alone reads +0.0132; linking faces through every welded edge instead merges it with a touching box into one shell of +0.0066, and reversing that shell moves the inversion onto the healthy box. With every normal turned to `dot = −0.25` from its face, it reads 0 % with 0 of 48 faces agreeing.

## Offline checks do not clear a spawn

Offline `.p3d` checks (a py3d reload, a per LOD digest, the normal budget, resolved vertices against 65,535, winding parity, indices, degenerate faces, a Geometry LOD identical to one that spawns) do not show that a model spawns. Spawn it in game before calling it ready.

- A helicopter model passed all of those (24,404 normals under 32,768, 46,905 resolved vertices under 65,535, winding 100 %, a Geometry LOD byte for byte equal to one that spawns) and still logged `Won't simulate, it has no geometry`. Cutting its normals to 5,506 did not change that, so the normal budget was not the cause.
- That message is the same defect as `Too many vertices`: the engine aborts loading the MLOD before it builds physics, and a valid Geometry LOD does not clear the model. On that helicopter 46,133 resolved triples load and 46,134 do not (25 in-game verdicts, none contradicting); the record is in `_shared/vehicles/binarize-vertex-budget.md`. Its 46,905 are over that limit, and `RESOLVED_LIMIT = 65535` misleads.
- Run `binarize` before touching geometry. It gives three results: PASS, CAPACITY_FAIL and OTHER_FAIL. On CAPACITY_FAIL do not go in game to fix the Geometry LOD; on OTHER_FAIL do not touch geometry at all. If `binarize` passes and the game still reports no geometry, other causes remain and the spawn decides. Do not go back to suspecting how the Visual LOD is written (corner order, tags, the normal pool) as the first cause, and do not treat the count as irrelevant.

### Reusable bisection

Put N test class names in one PBO, each with its own `.p3d` variant (LODs swapped with py3d); one restart then tests N variants, because file patching does not reload `.p3d` binaries. Give each class name a `CfgModels` entry that inherits the skeleton and crew bones of the real model: without it, a config with crew `proxyPos` bones crashed the server inside `CreateObjectEx` instead of returning a failed spawn. This is separate from the render side question ("Render side A/B" below). See also the vehicles pack (`_shared/vehicles/`) for get in and spawn, and the models pack (`_shared/models/`) for `.p3d` export.

## From Check B to fix

Isolate the minority group and flip it whole, reading the stored normals as Check A does. The method fixed 156 inverted faces across 11 parts of three gun rack models, seen in game as planks with inverted normals.

1. **Isolate.** Weld points by position (5 decimals), split the Visual LOD into connected components (leaving out faces of `proxy:*` selections), and in each component run an orientation flood fill with the Check B rule: two faces that traverse a shared two-face edge in the same direction are opposite. Then count the component's conflicts: two-face edges whose faces ended in the same group though they traverse the edge the same way, or in opposite groups though they traverse it opposite ways. A component with no conflict gives one group when healthy and two when broken, and the minority group is the inverted one: the reference is the part's own majority, never an absolute sign. A component with a conflict, or one that splits evenly, has no minority group, because where a conflicted fill splits depends on the order of the fill. Inspect it with the visibility check of step 2; its edges traversed the same way mark where to look.
   - Measured offline on the test car door's Visual LOD (healthy, right in game): it has one edge traversed the same way. With it, the fill splits a 2,235-face component 2,121 and 114 with 7 conflicts, and over 41 other face orders the minority ran from 6 to 246 faces, never none; with that edge cut, no conflict and no minority.
   - With a 30-face patch of that component's paint reversed in memory, the fill returned 144 faces (the patch and the 114). Cutting every edge traversed the same way, keeping cut those with the same piece on both sides, and filling again returned exactly the patch, behind 22 cut edges, and exactly the 29 damaged faces of a ring around a healthy face; but when the damaged ring took in a face of the door's one stray edge, it returned four intact faces and none of the 29. No fill decides such a component.
   - The three gun rack models have no conflict: their minority groups are the 156 faces the fix reversed, and two of them also hold two one-and-one splits each.
2. **Rank by visibility, not by count.** Render with an ID buffer and screen culling, with the sign calibrated on the model as a whole (the majority of a model that looks right in game must come out front facing). A minority group visible from outside is a defect players report; interior groups (shelf edges) take the same fix with lower urgency. Signed volume does not decide orientation on open sheets. Measure leftovers in pixels of visible backface, not in faces: one face made a strip of 636 px. A model identical to the shipped one is not cleared by that; a shipped asset can carry the defect from the start. A real, acceptable leftover is front-dominant faces with small slits of backface (a healthy model measured 4,335 px front and 59 back).
3. **Read the group's stored normals, then fix it.** Before touching the group, read each face with Check A, `dot(n_face, average of its corner normals)` with both normalized, where `n_face` is the face's vector area, the sum of `cross(v[j] − v[0], v[j+1] − v[0])` over its fan. On a triangle or a planar convex quad it points like `n_winding`; on a non-planar or non-convex quad the first three corners can point the other way, and which three they are changes with the reversal, while the vector area turns exactly (measured offline: a planar dart quad and a twisted quad sent the first-three reading down the wrong branch both ways, and the vector area sent all four right).

   The branches below are written for a part whose normals agree with its winding (rows 1 and 2 of the Check A table). Decide the row of the group's part (welded component, then material) first, from its faces outside the group, with the table and its direction test, never from the normals alone: a correct part whose normals were all turned reads like the older convention. A part with no face outside the group, or whose other faces read MIXED, has no row: inspect it. Bring the part to the map's convention with its row's fix, run step 1 again (a fix that reverses faces can repair the group with them), and read the group it then finds as written. A part kept in the older convention on purpose, checked in game, is read against that convention: the two branches swap, −0.5 or less meaning winding and normals turned together and +0.5 or more the winding alone.
   - Measured offline on a 30-face patch of the door's paint (a 1,926-face part, 98.5 % below −0.5), damaged in memory: every face read −1.000 with winding and normals reversed and +1.000 with the winding alone. The branches as written left 90 of 90 corners against the undamaged normals both times, while bringing the part to the convention first, or reading it against its kept convention, restored 30 of 30.
   - On a flat correct box with one face reversed and every normal turned, the other five faces read UNIFORM_FLIPPED with their cross product toward the box's centre (third row). That face read against them was reversed only and left 24 of 24 corners against the undamaged normals, with Check B clean; the box brought to the convention first came back corner for corner. With that face a part of its own, reversed with its normals or alone (rows 2 and 4), its row's fix restored the box, and reversing the old group as well took Check B to 4.

   Once every face of the group has a reading, invert the vertex order of every face of the group (`v[:1] + reversed(v[1:])`), and per face:
   - **Normals agree with the inverted winding** (reading +0.5 or more: they turned with it; second row): negate the stored normals of those corners in the same pass, unless the pipeline recalculates normals in a later step. This is the gun rack case: each minority group had its winding and its normals reversed against its neighbours', and the fixers refused to save unless no flipped face had summed normals against its new winding. A fixer that only inverts vertices is right only because its pipeline recalculates normals afterwards; copied to a pipeline that does not, it leaves the face visible but shaded inside out.
   - **Normals disagree with the inverted winding** (reading −0.5 or less: only the winding was reversed, and the normals still point like the neighbours'; fourth row): keep them; the new vertex order alone makes them agree. Measured offline on a correct unit box with one face reversed and its normals untouched: negating them as well left 5 of 6 faces agreeing (83.3 %, in py3d's absolute check and in Check A), while keeping them restored 6 of 6, corner for corner. Both left no edge traversed the same way. The same held with smoothed normals (one pool entry per point, shared by three faces) and for a two-face group with one face in each state: 83.3 % after negating both, 100 % after deciding face by face.
   - **No reading**: a face between −0.5 and +0.5 (normals near its plane, where the sign is noise and a change of 1e-15 picks the other branch), or with a corner on the other side of it or within 0.1 of its plane (`|dot| < 0.1` against `n_face`; an average can hide one such corner, as three agreeing corners and one at 1e-15 average to 0.9487). Inspect it, never infer its normals from the rest of the group. One face with no reading stops the whole group before any face is inverted. Measured offline: 16 of the 114 faces of the door's false split have no reading (glass: a corner on the other side on 13, within 0.1 on 3); stopping the group leaves the healthy door as it was, while fixing the other 98 alone takes Check B from 1 to 20 edges traversed the same way. It stops real groups too: a 30-face patch of the door's cabin plastic (99.5 % agreeing), damaged both ways, has one face at ±0.392, and the other 29 read the right branch.

   With the shared normal pool: if the `normal_index` entries of the corners to negate belong only to them, negate in place; if any is shared with a corner whose normal you keep (a face outside the group, or a face of the group that keeps its normals), add the negated normal as a new pool entry (32,768 budget) and re-index only those corners.

   Close on the whole LOD, not on the group: Check B finds no edge traversed the same way by both faces, and every corner normal points along its face's vector area by the same margin (`dot ≥ 0.1`), or against it (`dot ≤ −0.1`) on a part kept in the older convention, where most corners fail `dot ≥ 0.1` by construction. Inspect a corner that fails; never negate it on this reading alone, because a normal smoothed across a sharp fold can point against one of its faces with every face wound right. py3d's absolute check reads one corner per face and the group's reading sees only the group, so neither sees corners spoiled outside it.
   - Measured offline on the smoothed box with the reversed face's normals negated in place on entries its four neighbours share: the face was repaired with four pool copies and 8 corners of the neighbours were left pointing outward. py3d read 100 % with no winding finding (each neighbour's first corner was intact), while the corner check found the 8 and Check A read 33.3 %.
   - A flat tetrahedron wound inward with area-weighted corner normals fails the corner check at 6 of its 12 corners while healthy. On the healthy door, `dot ≥ 0.1` alone fails 26,576 of 35,766 corners, 25,934 of them on faces reading −0.5 or less; read against the face on its 11 parts that read UNIFORM_FLIPPED, as if all were kept on purpose, 441. A strip of 20 quads with the normals of 19 turned reads 95 % with 4 of its 80 corners at `dot ≥ 0.1`, so the label does not fix every corner.
4. **The defect comes back.** It measured identical across three successive deliveries of the same model: it lives in the working file, and exporting again does not cure it. Run the check on every delivery, not only the first import, and anchor the fixer so it fails closed (expected component centres and the exact face count): a model broken down differently on export then aborts instead of flipping the wrong faces.
5. **Inverted faces with no minority.** Edges and chamfers that share no two-face edge with their part are invisible to the flood fill of step 1. Find them by back dominance over a set of views covering the whole sphere: per face, at least 8 pixels won in the depth buffer as backface, and at least four times those won as front. A second pass on the gun racks found 17 more such faces, with strips up to 636 px, that the first pass had dismissed as leftovers.
   - Do not judge by position: "near the top, so the outside is +Y" errs on hanging faces (soffits, shelf bottoms).
   - Do not judge by two-way raycast: it cannot resolve sheets about 1 mm apart, where the ray hits the twin skin in both directions.
   - Check the view set itself: in the usual yaw and pitch projection of these renderers a positive pitch lowers the camera, so a "top view" that looks from below leaves the roof unchecked.
   - Close the loop with a full render after the fix: no back-dominant face, and none new.

## Render side A/B and source profile limits

Edge pair coherence and the ratio of cross product to stored normal test internal consistency; they do not predict which side the engine will draw. Normals and winding can agree with each other while both use the wrong sign for the target pipeline.

When the absolute sign stays uncertain, make a variant with every Visual face reversed, proxy triangles left out because their winding encodes the proxy frame. Binarize the candidate and the variant, then compare them in game. Before spending that cycle, measure both outputs with the same count and require the results to differ; equal results make the comparison worthless. Binarize does not normalize per face winding: it turns the sign at the MLOD to ODOL boundary (`winding-absolute-check.md`, "The sign flips between MLOD and ODOL"), so a flipped variant stays distinct after binarizing.

A dot count before binarizing predicts the render side only inside a calibrated source profile. Leave out dots near zero, do not compare ratios across vanilla ODOL, converted MLOD and raw MLOD from a modelling tool, and do not carry a flip rule from a generic export over to a vehicle whose source transform measured differently. After adopting a flip, recalibrate every expected share in the assembler's checks. The in-game comparison stays the verdict on the render side.
