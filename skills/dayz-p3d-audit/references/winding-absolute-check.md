# Reading py3d's winding findings

How to read the winding findings `audit_p3d.py` prints from the DayZ fork of py3d (`ERR_WINDING_INVERTED`, `ERR_WINDING_VS_NORMALS`, `WARN_WINDING_NORMAL_MISMATCH`), why a clean py3d result is not proof of correct geometry, and how the winding sign changes between the MLOD and the binarized ODOL. The check methods are in `winding-methodology.md`; the state table and the fix procedure are in `winding-diagnostics.md`.

Everything here was measured on mod models, in game or offline with py3d, and is not checked on `P:\`: vanilla models are binarized and py3d cannot read them. The vanilla lines that say which ray each consumer casts were opened: the action cursor casts `RaycastRVProxy` (`P:\scripts\4_world\classes\useractionscomponent\actiontargets.c:255`), and hologram placement casts `RaycastRV` with `ObjIntersectFire` (`P:\scripts\4_world\classes\hologram.c:683`).

The Blender to DayZ map and the per component winding check are the sections of those names in `_shared/models/generated-model-rules.md`. After the map, the cross product `cross(v1-v0, v2-v0)` and the stored normals both point away from the side meant to be seen; the per component check requires every face of a closed, convex collision component to read inward. The py3d README is in `scripts/tools/py3d/`.

## The three py3d gates

A clean `validate()`, a passing `save(verify=True)` or a `diff` that reports equal does not show that the geometry is correct; those checks prove that counts agree. When you call a model verified, say whether its vertex order was compared against debinarized vanilla or tested in game.

1. `validate()` does not see a global winding inversion: its winding check is relative to the Visual LOD. If every LOD is inverted, it returns an empty list and exit 0. If only the Visual LOD is inverted, it accuses the healthy collision LODs (`ERR_WINDING_INVERTED`). The message names the Visual LOD it compares against, says the LODs disagree, not which one is wrong, and gives the winding steps for both (py3d README, "Winding"). Measured offline with the Visual LOD turned inside out, faces and normals together: the finding named the three healthy collision LODs; reversing all their faces traded it for `ERR_WINDING_VS_NORMALS`, and negating their normals as well left `validate()` empty with every LOD wound outward, broken and silent.
2. `save(verify=True)` does not compare geometry. It checks counts, selection names and the mass sum; points at `(0,0,0)` and at `(99,99,99)` both verify.
3. `python -m py3d diff` reporting `total: 0` does not prove the geometry equal; the same pair reports `total: 0`, exit 0.

What does discriminate on a full LOD set: edge coherence (neighbours traverse a shared edge in opposite directions) and `cross(e1,e2) . declared_normal` in the same space.

## What 0 % agreement means

The fork's absolute check, `_check_winding_absolute`, takes `_pct_normal_agreement(lod)`, the share of faces with `dot(cross(v1−v0, v2−v0), declared_normal_v0) > 0`, both vectors in raw MLOD space. It raises `ERR_WINDING_VS_NORMALS` (CRITICAL) near 0 % and `WARN_WINDING_NORMAL_MISMATCH` when mixed. Read on the Visual LOD:

| MLOD | Status | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| A shipped solar panel model (2,944 faces) | in use, seen in game | 100.0 % |
| A shipped kit box model (12 faces) | in use | 100.0 % |
| A test car's co-driver door (double-sided glass with negated normals) | right in game | 26 % |
| A third-party Blender door export (11 LODs) | normals inverted, winding correct | 0 % in every visual and collision LOD |
| A third-party Blender room export (12 LODs) | normals inverted, winding correct | 0 % in every visual and collision LOD |

So 0 % means winding and stored normals disagree, not that the winding is wrong. A build that reversed every face of both exports on that reading rendered them inside out (textures visible only from inside; the room's Roadway faces flipped too), and `RaycastRVProxy` in `view`, `fire` and `geom` from 2 m in front of the door found nothing while a terrain control hit.

A measure that ignores the normals settles the direction: the signed volume by winding of the Visual LOD (the divergence sum over fan triangles, in raw MLOD space). It reads −0.1145 on the kit box, −0.0691 on the solar panel, −0.1967 on the door export (the same sign as the shipped models) and +25.19 on the room export (a room seen from inside, faces toward the interior). The face-reversed builds carry the opposite signs.

## Reading rules

1. A healthy single-sided MLOD reads about 100 %. 0 % means winding and stored normals disagree and does not say which is wrong. Never take the fix direction from this number alone. The relative check cannot see a global disagreement; this one can.
2. A mixed percentage is not an inversion. On a model with double-sided twins it can be the twins voting; isolate the minority group per welded component (`winding-diagnostics.md`) instead of flipping everything. On the test car's door the twins do not explain the mix: coincident twins are 144 of 11,921 faces, and without them the count is unchanged (72.7 % flipped, 25.9 % agreeing). The vote splits by part (exterior paint, black trim and mirror against their normals; cabin trim and glass with them), and no face group is inverted: Check B finds one stray edge, and with it cut every welded component orients as one group (`winding-diagnostics.md`, Check A, `MIXED`).
3. Measure in raw MLOD coordinates. A frame that already flips Z (an OBJ export, `parse_obj` helpers) inverts the sign.
4. Decide the direction with the signed volume by winding. A solid seen from outside reads negative on shipped models (a convex box also winds 0 % of its faces outward from its centroid); a room meant to be seen from inside reads positive; Roadway faces walkable from above read `cross_Y < 0`, the same side as the kit box's top face. An export whose sign matches the shipped models has the winding right and the normals wrong.
5. Fix the side that is wrong.
   - Normals wrong: keep the winding and negate the normal pool in place, `lod.facenormals[j] = (-x, -y, -z)`, never through the `Vertex.normal` setter, which re-indexes into the pool. Negate the whole pool only when every part of the LOD reads the third row of the Check A table, as both third-party exports did; otherwise negate only the parts on that row, through a negated copy, re-indexed, of any entry a corner you keep also uses (`winding-diagnostics.md`, "From Check B to fix"). The audit then reads about 100 % with every face order as in the source.
   - Winding wrong: `face.vertices.reverse()` only on the faces the direction check of their kind reads wrong, never a `vertices[1]`/`[2]` swap (a quad becomes a crossed face). A collision component: the faces the per component check reads outward, its prerequisites met (`killers-detail.md`, "Collision LOD wound outward"). A Visual LOD: shell by shell, after Check B has turned the faces wound against their neighbours, every face of a closed shell whose signed volume has the wrong sign for the side meant to be seen; an open or double-sided part has no sign. Roadway: the walkable faces against rule 4's sign.
   - Then read the stored normals of the faces you reverse, before the reversal, with the Check A table, part by part: negate them where the part's normals agreed with its wrong winding (second row), keep them where they disagreed (fourth row), read a group inside a part face by face, and leave a part or face with no clean reading for inspection. Never negate a corner on its own sign; smoothed normals can point against a face that is right. The fork under `scripts/tools/py3d/` prints the collision and Visual winding steps with these findings, and its closing normals step goes part by part (py3d README, "Winding", step 3).
   - Reverse every face of every LOD only inside a whole model operation: undoing the reversal that `P3D.transform()` applies for a matrix with a negative determinant (`py3d.blender_to_dayz()` does it), or rule 8's reflection.
   - Confirm in the engine (outside render, inside render, raycast, walking the Roadway) before you adopt a new fix rule. The negate-normals build of the two third-party exports passed: door and room render right side out from outside and inside, and `RaycastRVProxy` in `view` and `fire` hit the door from front and back and the room wall from both sides, while the face-reversed build missed every ray.

   Why the scope matters, measured offline on synthetic 2 m boxes whose normals agree with their winding:

   - A correct exterior Visual LOD (signed volume −8) over a Geometry box wound outward (+8): reversing every face of both ends at Visual +8 and Geometry −8, still raises `ERR_WINDING_INVERTED`, and raises `ERR_WINDING_VS_NORMALS` on both LODs unless every normal is negated too. Reversing the six Geometry faces only, their normals negated, leaves −8 and −8 and no winding finding.
   - A healthy closed box (−8) and an inverted one (+8) in one Visual LOD share six pool entries. Reversing the inverted box leaves 24 of 48 corners agreeing (`WARN_WINDING_NORMAL_MISMATCH`); negating the whole pool then still leaves 24 of 48, with the healthy box's normals turned; negating the inverted box's normals alone through six negated copies leaves 48 of 48. With that box's winding right and its normals turned instead (third row), the whole pool again leaves 24 of 48 and that part alone 48 of 48.
   - A flat tetrahedron wound inward with area-weighted normals has 6 of its 12 corners against their faces while healthy. With every face reversed, its part reads no clean row and the winding repair alone restores it; negating each corner that points against its face would turn 6 of its 12 normals.
6. `ERR_WINDING_INVERTED` does not come from the absolute check. py3d raises it in `_check_winding_vs_visual`, the relative check, which compares each collision LOD's share of faces whose cross product points away from the centroid of the LOD's points with the Visual LOD's share. On the shipped kit box the absolute check stays silent (winding and normals agree on every face), while the relative check fires on the Geometry, View and Fire LODs: each holds one `Component01` of 12 triangles, all wound outward (+0.1145), against an inward Visual LOD (−0.1145). The per component check reads them outward too.
   - In game, packed unbinarized as shipped, that collision took 0 of 27 rays: nine per mode in `geom`, `view` and `fire` (from above, from the four sides at mid height, from one side at two other heights, and two from inside). A ray built like the vanilla cursor ray (`RaycastRVProxy`, `ObjIntersectView`) passed through it to the ground; the probe casts it as a 0.05 m sphere, where the vanilla ray has radius 0. In the same run a shipped model with six components wound inward, and a vanilla `WoodenCrate` (the kit's own `item_small` layer), took 21 of 21.
   - With only the collision LODs changed, all 12 faces of each reversed and the Visual and Memory LODs untouched, the kit took 27 of 27 and the cursor-like ray stopped on its top, whether its collision normals were negated with the winding or kept. The shipped file took 0 of 27 again, and the `WoodenCrate` 27 of 27. Both fixed versions answered every ray at the same coordinates, so a collision LOD's stored normals do not change these rays, and binarize writes both to the same ODOL. Negate them anyway, for the Blender to DayZ convention and a clean audit: with the shipped normals the absolute check raises `ERR_WINDING_VS_NORMALS` on the fixed LODs.
   - The outward winding did not hide the kit from the physics world: `DayZPhysics.RayCastBullet` found the shipped kit's top, east and north faces where it found the fixed kit's. That is a static query; player contact, standing on the kit and client collision were not measured, nor a real bullet or a hologram. The vanilla cursor and hologram rays use the same intersection modes at radius 0 with their own flags and checks, and some hologram rays are ground only and skip every object whatever its winding. A walking player went through the 0.49 m `item_small` kit fixed or not, so a walk tells nothing.
   - Treat that kit box's collision as broken and keep its collision LODs out of any calibration of healthy winding. Read `ERR_WINDING_INVERTED` as a trigger for the per component check, which decides, and fix only the faces that read outward. The finding also fires on healthy collision LODs under an inverted Visual LOD.
   - How to cast the same rays and controls in game is in `skills/dayz-launch-test/references/mcp/collision-probes.md`, "The set of rays and its controls": nine rays per mode, the same set on a control from the same PBO and on a vanilla control of the same physics layer, and a pair that changes only the winding before the winding is named as the cause. An item a player or `action_use` picks up has shown no collision: a player looking down can target it through a cone search that needs no hit, and `action_use` casts no ray (same file, "Picking an item up proves nothing about its collision").
7. Shipped collision LODs carry mixed whole LOD signs, while seven debinarized vanilla models and four shipped mod models wind 0 % outward per component. Never match a collision LOD to the Visual LOD. Keep the export's orientation only where every face of a closed, convex component reads inward; a component the check cannot score (open, planar or double-sided) is unresolved, not a sign to keep.
8. A sign that matches the shipped models does not rule out a mirrored export. The two third-party exports read 0 % with a correct-looking sign, and the negate-normals build rendered right side out as a mirror image: door handle and bed on the opposite side from the vendor's renders, lettering reversed. A reflection on export flips the winding, and an exporter that then reverses the vertex order hides it. Before choosing the fix, compare one asymmetric feature (handle side, furniture layout, lettering) with the vendor's render. To fix a mirrored export, reflect x on every point of every LOD (Memory points included), reverse the vertex order of every face, and map the normal pool to `(nx, -ny, -nz)`. The audit then reads 100 % with the shipped sign, collision still hits, and the asymmetric feature lands where the render puts it.

## The sign flips between MLOD and ODOL

On the test car's binarize path, triangles of the source MLOD and the published ODOL paired by centroid had opposite orientation in 12,784 of 12,784 pairs. A cull or winding check calibrated on the MLOD must invert its sign to audit the ODOL of that path; for another toolchain, measure the boundary again instead of assuming it.

Prove a release check on a known MLOD and ODOL pair, and show that it separates a healthy build from a deliberately broken one. The same zero count across very different inputs means the instrument failed, not that the builds are clean: a check read 0 of 2,367 on three different builds, and with the sign inverted ODOL and MLOD agreed cell by cell (637 to 0 and 636 to 0). Check the published ODOL, and never carry the MLOD check over from memory.

## Finding a see-through from a vehicle seat

Reproduce a defect the player reports from the seat from the real first person camera, not an estimated eye: an eye 23 cm below and behind the real one showed neither of two see-through patches at the windshield base. One real camera is still one sample; where the eye moves with the character, the speed and the pitch, test from every eye the rider can have (`_shared/vehicles/view-pilot-closures.md`).

1. Recover the camera from the user's own screenshot by solving the camera pose from dashboard features at known positions (a reprojection error of 2.7 px was reached).
2. Render the LOD with a depth buffer and flag every pixel whose nearest opaque face is a backface. Measure the seal this way: in the measured case, 2,203 inverted twins brought the see-through pixels to 0.
3. Show the before and after to the user before touching geometry.
4. Seal the culprits with inverted twins (same points, reversed vertex order and UVs, negated normals), never by flipping. Leave out twin candidates that lie within 2 mm of another piece, or they flicker.

Telling a winding fault from a hole still takes the ray set of rule 6 and the per component winding check.
