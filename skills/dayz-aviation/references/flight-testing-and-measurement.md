# Flight testing and measurement

Working rules for testing a scripted flight model in game and judging the result from logs: replay and handshake rules for a predicted aircraft, how to judge a control loop, phase gates, why a diagnostic probe may be blind to stutter, and how to score time windows across client and server logs.

Measured results and working rules from the LFHeli helicopter project, a third-party `CarScript` helicopter mod by the author of these notes. Not yet checked on `P:\` except where a `P:\` path is cited. `LFHeli...` file and line references point into that project's source and review files, which are not in this repo; they are kept so the numbers can be traced. The `LL-nnn` labels are that project's lesson numbers.

## Rules from flight campaigns

- **LL-194, replay state.** List every field the solver or state machine reads during a replay and class it as restored, recomputed after the handshake, or always initialised. Never initialise gain values or derived tables only inside an `IsServer` branch.
- **LL-195, handshakes.** Do not use a one shot handshake when it depends on crew, possession or spawn state that is still asynchronous. Retry from the client until it is acknowledged, or push from the server when the state is ready. Compare identities by a stable ID, not by instance.
- **LL-201, reading reconciliation.** Diagnose reconciliation with aligned series. Sawteeth mean corrections followed by new divergence. Steady growth or a plateau without resets means the transform is not being corrected. Find the event that triggers convergence before changing the solver.
- **LL-233, pulses behind a threshold.** For each pulse that fires only past a threshold, list every writer of the same variable in that window and work out whether the precondition can be reached when the pulse fires. If a continuous actuator converges the error before the pulse (here dead reckoning blending at about `0.35` per frame against a required pre-error of at least 0.5 m/s), open a coast window that suspends the actuator. A pulse that does not fire is recorded as data with its own reason and does not invalidate the whole window. The live harness with the mitigation is at `LFHeli_SF8B/scripts/4_World/LFHeliVariants.c:937-991` (`CampaignPulseIsCoasting` at `:941` and `:963-968`, the low pre-error no-fire at `:984-988`).
- **LL-291, judge the loop by output, target and difference.** The shape of the control law alone proves nothing. Log per tick the raw law output, the target, the output actually applied and `out - target`; inspect the clamps after the law before touching the law. A residue equal to a tuning constant is saturation, not dynamics: here a residue of 9.0 degrees was `AutoHoverMaxTiltDeg=9` capping the target before `TrackNormalRollTarget` (`LFHeliCore/scripts/4_world/LFHeli/LFHeliFlightModel.c:169-178`).
- **LL-292, calibrate the pre-state gate against a real pilot.** Before adding a prohibition, read how the previous clean windows were obtained, copy their conditions literally, and do not add an axis "just in case", because that changes the physics being measured. Here a manual hover reaches 3.5 degrees or less, and 1 degree or less only after a recover; an extra forward speed ban cost a whole measurement cell.
- **LL-299, the scripted gate matches the registered one, and "settled" is read from height.** The gate in script must be byte identical to the one registered before the test. Detect "settled" by height above ground, not by velocity. Do not add a forward speed limit of 0.5 m/s when the registration asks only for roll and sideways speed. Do not use the owner's `GetVelocity()` (declared at `P:\scripts\1_core\proto\enphysics.c:104`) on the ground: there it can report the commanded velocity (measured -1.96 m/s with the helicopter still and a contact distance of 0.0003). Use a sustained, independent height above ground condition and release the collective below 1 m.
- **LL-295, the Pawn move is already the scripted pilot channel.** When the server consumes the owner's move, inject the axes between the local read and the move write inside `ObtainMove`. Keep the normal path byte identical while the override is inactive, and log entry and exit. In LFHeli the hook sits between `ReadRawLocal` and `WriteToMove` (`LFHeliCore/scripts/4_world/LFHeli/LFHeli_Base.c:2290-2306`; signatures at `LFHeliInputs.c:175,219`). Do not add an RPC or clock sync for data that already travels that frame.
- **LL-298, normalise replay and clocks before judging.** Rewind and replay can emit several samples with the same tick and make flags flicker on edges: keep the first sample per tick (here 8 to 21 percent were duplicates). Client and server timestamps do not share an origin; measure the offset with pairs of the same event (`LEVEL` and `RECGATE`, `EXIT` and `EXIT`, here 31 to 34 s) and align them. Publish the authority series beside the aggregate and use it for the verdict.
- **LL-308, phase gates are decided by the authority.** The owner's presentation is supporting evidence (this refines LL-299). When the server has published `GROUND_READY` or `PARKED`, that state closes the landing. The owner's height above ground can bounce through interpolation over a still authority; it is a fallback and a witness, and must not reopen a confirmed phase. Record both signals over the same interval (`LFHeliCore/scripts/4_world/LFHeli/LFHeliScriptedPilot.c:575-594`) and apply the same authority review to `LEVEL` and `PRESTATE`.

## A diagnostic probe is not a smoothness instrument

Before using any existing probe to judge "smooth", "no stutter" or "no lag", and before freezing a baseline computed from it, open the probe's emission site and answer three questions.

1. **What is its emission period?** A probe written to measure a constant (an offset, a drift, a steady residual) is normally throttled to seconds. LFHeli's `[LFHELI-REF]` emits when `refProbeNow - m_RefProbeLastPeriodicLog >= 2.0` (`LFHeliCore\scripts\4_world\LFHeli\LFHeli_Base.c:2203`), which is 0.5 Hz, with a cap of 10 lines per 1 s window (`:2205`).
2. **What threshold makes it fire out of turn?** `[LFHELI-REF]` breaks its period only when `refStateBodyDistance > 6.0` (`:2204`). The stutters under investigation measured 2.3 to 3.9 m, so they pass under the trigger and never fire the probe.
3. **Can it resolve the thing being judged at all?** A jolt that lasts one frame, sampled every 2 s, is invisible by construction. An earlier LFHeli research pass had written this caveat about its own 5 Hz probe ("does not show whether the change happens in one or several frames", `LFHeli_dev\reviews\2026-08-03-sf8b-research-codex.md:79`), and it was still overlooked a week later.

**The frozen baseline inherits the blindness.** LFHeli's flight baseline was computed over 70 samples in 159 s (`reviews\2026-08-12-investigacion-offset-codex.md:274`), which is exactly that throttled probe. An A/B against it is a fair comparison (same instrument on both sides) and still cannot answer "did the jolts go away", which is the product question. Both statements are true at once, and a review that picks only one of them judges wrongly.

**Rule.** Run both streams in a measurement cell: the original probe at its original cadence, so the A/B against the frozen baseline stays valid, and a per frame continuity stream (position and angle delta and jerk per frame) that can see a one frame discontinuity. Do not make the second stream conditional on the first one's outcome: when the first is blind to the problem, its outcome cannot be the gate that allows measuring properly.

**Measurement mode against release mode.** Raising a probe's cadence and lowering its trigger for a measurement cell is a parameter change, not new instrumentation; a "no new instrumentation" boundary should not block it. The reverse also holds: never strip diagnostic probes from a build before the measurement campaign that reads them has closed, or the campaign silently loses its instrument.

## Scoring an attitude window across client and server logs

A parser that checks owner time windows against server events (`RECGATE`, `TDGATE`, `GND`) must move the server clock onto the client clock before scoring the band.

1. **Clock offset.** Measure it from pairs of the same event: `EXIT` and `EXIT`, or `LEVEL` on the client and the first `RECGATE` on the server. Here it was about 31 to 34 s. Without the offset a correct window comes out invalid, and a real violation can fall outside the band.
2. **Remove the owner's replay samples.** An owner that predicts with the Pawn pipeline (rewind and replay) emits the probe several times per frame with the same time `t` (8 to 21 percent of the samples). On hold flanks the roll activity flag flickers across those copies and the parser reports a false "pre-state has roll active" or "reactivation at +0.001 s". Keep the first sample per tick (or within 20 ms): that one is the present, the rest are replays.
3. **Judge on the authority first.** Prefer the authority series, which has no replays, and treat the owner series as secondary. After changing the parser, run the historical cells again and check that they do not move; here two earlier cells stayed identical.

The parser is `LFHeli_dev/tools/ah2_windows.py` version 2 (with `--offset` and the duplicate removal), in the LFHeli project.
